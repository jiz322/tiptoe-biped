Phase 2 — Filters closed spd_filt hypothesis

M2.1 verdict — three independent measurements agree The RS01 spd_filt_gain=0.1 EMA runs at the current-loop rate (~13 kHz): a 0.70 ms, |H|≈1.00 filter, flat to 25 Hz, corner ≈230 Hz — transparent where the balance loop lives. This one measurement carries the verdict, and it is enough.) (3) kd brake test: undershoot 3.4% at kd=2, none at kd=0.5, against a ≥13.6% counterfactual. (Review: 3.4% sits under 2 velocity LSBs and ~6× below the run's own 22% noise floor; the two kd conditions are statistically indistinguishable and the counterfactual collapses to ~0 at the engaged inertia. Withdrawn.)
  • M2.2 (firmware A/B): moot. Nothing to raise; sim and firmware are already "matched" — the filter is negligible on both sides. No vendor-tool writes.
  • M2.3 (EL05 loc_ref_filt_gain=0.04): not live in MIT mode. Position-step responses show no 20–50 ms filter. (Review 08-18: the published “1.7% rms, filter fitted to exactly 0 ms” does not reproduce — refit gives 21.6% rms with the prefilter pole pinned at its bound.   • Bonus physics retracted 08-18 — as published: hip_pitch inertia 8.48e-3 kg·m², “independently validating” the cfg's 8.4e-3 combined armature, pendulum stiffness 4.73 Nm/rad (3.8 Hz). Do not use. Methodology notes for the remaining phases

  • Hand-held-base data breaks rigid-plant fits (FRF chirp 28% rms, step control-fit 32%). If a test needs the torso held, design it so the discriminator lives in the first ~10 ms (brake test), or fixture the robot.
  • Time-domain bounding beat FRF fitting on this rig: stiction (C ≈ 0.1–0.3 Nm, amplitude-dependent) and a rest equilibrium only known to ±40 mrad poison small-signal frequency-domain work.
  • Synthetic self-tests first: the kd fit pipeline was validated on simulated data with known truth before touching real data — kept a sign-convention bug from becoming a wrong conclusion.

Phase 5 — Friction & inertia bench closed (session 3)

All captures tether-hung, commanded-τ only (June's friction_id.py is superseded — it used torque_fdb as truth). Two protocols, both synthetic-validated before real data: a slow τ-ramp breakaway (gravity cancels by direction symmetry) and an M3.1-envelope ballistic battery (coast_down_bench.py --mode ballistic: strictly alternating short bursts, levels 1–4 Nm, logged PD brake, offset-start reps). Fit: data/coastdown_fit.py burst-primary regression, 8 selftest cases.

Headline — the 19.8 vs ≥43e-3 inertia conflict is method bias, not physics Burst traces exposed a lash-engagement onset transient on hip_pitch (first ~20 ms the rotor accelerates alone at ~τ/12e-3 through gear lash, then dumps momentum into the leg). Synthetic replay proved (a) the M3.1 naive slope method inflates I under damping+lash (a planted 20e-3 reads 42.9), and (b) the kp-sweep's small-signal ring rides the rotor side of the lash. The two-mass (rotor–lash–leg) character itself is now a live candidate for the un-caught plant gap.

What survives verification

  • Engaged inertia is solid. Pooling the two well-conditioned hip_pitch ballistic runs (bal2+bal3, 926 burst samples, 9 torque levels each direction) gives I = 31.0e-3 with a rep-bootstrap 5–95 CI of 29.6–32.8e-3 (±5%). Knee 20.7e-3 (CI 19.6–21.8). The P5 headline figures are reproducible.
  • M1.3 soak reproduces exactly from the retained per-tick CSV: 59,992 ticks, 100.0000% six-of-six replies, busy p50 2.371 ms, one 95.8 ms stall.

Unit convention — the write-time hazard for Phase 7

Isaac Lab's armature, friction, dynamic_friction, viscous_friction are per-DOF joint-space tensors (actuator_base.py:94-104). Every P5 number above is motor-space. hip_pitch and hip_roll are direct-drive so they transfer unchanged, but the knee needs G = 22/30 = 0.7333: Ijoint = I/G² (×1.860), Cjoint = C/G (×1.364), bjoint = b/G² (×1.860). A naive write is wrong by those factors — knee C 0.130 → 0.177, knee b 0.016 → 0.029, knee I 20.7e-3 → 38.5e-3. The cfg already uses joint-space with exactly these reflections (knee armature 7.81e-3 = 4.2e-3/G², saturation_effort 23.2 = 17/G, velocity_limit 20 = 27.5·G), which is how the convention was confirmed. belt_knee_actuator.py contains no G at all and offers no protection: whatever is written into the knee column is interpreted as knee joint properties, and c enters only the velocity used for the DC envelope, never the friction or armature terms.

Two traps follow. (1) Knee friction 0.06 and dynamic_friction 0.05 sit below hip's 0.12/0.10 where the reflection demands above — a pre-existing wrong-direction belt conversion (0.12 × 0.5), inconsistent with the armature convention three lines up. Recompute these; do not preserve the ratio. (2) The cfg's 8.4e-3 hip armature deliberately includes the knee rotor reflected through the belt anchor (c²·7.81e-3 ≈ 4.2e-3), and P5's engaged hip inertia was measured belt-on, so it already contains that same term. Writing Iengaged − Ilink while keeping the reflection counts the knee rotor twice.
